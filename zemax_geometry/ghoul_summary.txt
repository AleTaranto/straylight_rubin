1. BRIGHT-STAR ANNULUS TEST  (the primary question)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Question: is there always a bright star at some preferred off-axis distance
from the boresight when this artifact appears? Statistic: the number of
distinct NIGHTS on which at least one labelled exposure has a star in a given
annulus. Counting nights rather than exposures is what stops a single burst
from being read as hundreds of detections. The null circularly shifts each
night's label vector, which preserves run lengths and spacings, so the null
samples are exactly as bursty as the real one. The reported p is family-wise
over the whole scan: each cell is standardised, and the maximum z is
calibrated against the per-replicate maximum, so picking the best of 8795
cells costs what it should.

The scan covers brightness as well as radius. One magnitude cut will not do:
taking every star down to V=4.5 means that at 20 deg off axis almost every
exposure has one, so the indicator saturates and any real contrast is diluted
away. Brightness is also the physically right axis, since scattered flux
scales with the flux of the source.

2. WHICH FRAME IS THE FEATURE FIXED IN?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A stray-light feature is fixed in some frame, and each implicates a different
surface: the camera frame means the optics (a ghost), the dome frame means the
structure or the slit, the sky frame means the atmosphere -- or, far more
often, that the survey kept revisiting one field.

  stars per exposure in the winning annulus: mean 1.06, exactly one in 94 % of cases

This is why the frame cannot be identified from telemetry: with about one star
per exposure there is a single direction, and the three frames are then
related by fixed offsets (rotator, parallactic angle). Two stars at the same
radius differ in position angle by a rotator-invariant constant, so they
rotate together and add no discriminating power either. The concentrations
below are therefore DESCRIPTIVE; read their ordering, not their absolute size.

    frame     R(labels)  R(controls)   excess   mean direction
    camera        0.848        0.093    0.754   19 deg
    horizon       0.983        0.099    0.884   1 deg
    sky           0.459        0.189    0.270   228 deg

  'excess' is R(labels) - R(controls): each frame compared against its
  own baseline, which is the only fair comparison since the frames have
  different intrinsic concentrations.

  The camera frame shows the larger excess, which is the camera-fixed (ghost)
ordering. Suggestive only, and worth pursuing only if the annulus test above
survived its controls.
